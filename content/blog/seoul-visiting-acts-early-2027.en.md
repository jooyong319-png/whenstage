The first quarter of 2027 has an unusual shape for anyone following international artists into Korea. The headline stadium names are already familiar — [Maroon 5](/en/concert/en-maroon-5-asia-2027-seoul-20270127) play KINTEX in Goyang on January 27, and [Bruno Mars](/en/concert/en-bruno-mars-the-romantic-seoul-20270521) has six nights at Goyang Stadium in May. What is new is the layer underneath: two of Japan's biggest solo artists, a returning R&B favorite, an Irish songwriter marking an anniversary, and a British indie artist finally getting her first headline date.

These five shows have almost nothing in common musically. What makes them worth reading together is that each one is reached through a different ticket seller, with a different set of rules — and if you are booking from outside the country, those rules matter more than the setlist.

## Fujii Kaze: the dome, and the four-ticket cap

[Fujii Kaze's Prema World Tour](/en/concert/en-fujii-kaze-prema-world-tour-seoul-20270109) reaches [Gocheok Sky Dome](/en/venue/Gocheok%20Sky%20Dome%2C%20Seoul) on January 9, 2027 at 6 p.m. It is his largest Korean show so far, in the country's only domed stadium, which is a real statement for a Japanese singer-songwriter in a market where the dome is usually reserved for the biggest K-pop tours.

AEG Presents Asia promotes the date, and this is the one show here sold through Melon Ticket rather than NOL. Tickets went on sale on June 17, 2026, with a limit of four per buyer. Floor seats run from 187,000 to 210,000 won, with reserved seating from 132,000 won. The early on-sale date is the point to notice: this was a summer purchase for a January concert.

## Kenshi Yonezu: three nights by the airport

Five days later, [Kenshi Yonezu](/en/artist/Kenshi%20Yonezu) opens a three-night stand of his [GHOST world tour](/en/concert/en-kenshi-yonezu-ghost-world-tour-incheon-20270114) at [Inspire Arena](/en/venue/Inspire%20Arena%2C%20Incheon) near Incheon Airport — Thursday, January 14 at 7:30 p.m., then Saturday, January 16 and Sunday, January 17 at 7 p.m. KST. That is one more show than his sold-out 2025 Korean debut at the same arena, and Korea is the first overseas stop of the tour's 2027 leg.

Strictly speaking, this is not a Seoul concert, and visitors should plan for that. The upside of the location is obvious for anyone flying in: the arena sits next to the airport, not across the city. Tickets cost 143,000 to 172,000 won and have been on sale through NOL since October 22, including an English booking page on NOL World — the most foreign-friendly setup on this list.

## Jeff Bernat: one night, one seller

[Jeff Bernat](/en/artist/Jeff%20Bernat) returns to Korea for the first time in three years with a single show at YES24 Wanderloch Hall in Seodaemun, Seoul, on [Thursday, February 18, 2027](/en/concert/en-jeff-bernat-luv-language-tour-seoul-20270218), at 8 p.m. KST. Seoul sits between Taipei and Tokyo on his Luv Language Asia-Pacific run, and fans can expect 'Call You Mine' and 'Cruel' alongside newer material.

This is the smallest room of the five and the only one sold exclusively through YES24 Ticket, which opened on November 10 at noon KST. One date, one hall and one ticketing site make this the show where waiting carries the most risk.

## Damien Rice: a seated anniversary in Gangnam

[Damien Rice](/en/artist/Damien%20Rice) marks 25 years of his debut album O with [three all-seated shows](/en/concert/en-damien-rice-o-25th-anniversary-tour-seoul-20270227) at [GS Arts Center](/en/venue/GS%20Arts%20Center%2C%20Seoul) in Yeoksam — Saturday, February 27 at 7 p.m., then Sunday, February 28 at 2:30 p.m. and 7 p.m. KST. It is his first Korean stage since January 2025, and Seoul is one of 25 cities on the anniversary tour.

The Sunday matinee is worth singling out. A 2:30 p.m. start is rare for a touring Western artist in Seoul, and it suits anyone who wants a concert without a late-night journey back across the city. Seats cost 110,000 to 176,000 won. The artist presale closed on October 23 and general sale opened that day at noon, on both NOL and the GS Arts Center site.

## beabadoobee: a first headline show, at last

[beabadoobee](/en/artist/beabadoobee) plays her first headline show in Korea on [Tuesday, March 23, 2027](/en/concert/en-beabadoobee-powerlines-tour-seoul-20270323), at 8 p.m. KST at Olympic Hall in Olympic Park, after a planned festival appearance the year before fell through. The date belongs to the Powerlines Tour, her first arena tour, built around the album Pylon.

An artist presale ran October 14-15 and general sale opened October 16 at noon KST on NOL, which also lists the show on NOL World in English. Note the age rule: the show is for ages 12 and up.

## What the pattern tells you

Put the five side by side and the Seoul ticketing market looks less like one system and more like four. Melon Ticket handled the dome, YES24 Ticket holds the one-night club show, and NOL carries the rest, sometimes with an English page and sometimes alongside a venue's own site. Promoters differ too — AEG Presents Asia, Live Nation Korea, Eventim Live Korea and Private Curve all appear on this short list.

The practical lesson is simple. For a show in or around Seoul, check which site is selling it before you check the price, and check the on-sale date before either — several of these opened months before the concert.
