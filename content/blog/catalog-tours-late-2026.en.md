A tour normally exists because an album does. A record comes out, a routing follows, the setlist leans on the new songs and the old ones function as relief. Look at the last two months of 2026 and that relationship is inverted almost everywhere you look. The album, where there is one, is a compilation. The tour is named after something that already happened. The product being sold is the back catalog itself.

This is not the same thing as a nostalgia circuit, and it is worth separating the versions of it, because a catalog can be sold in at least four different ways and these acts are using all of them.

## The hits album as a tour engine

The cleanest example is [The Darkness](/en/artist/The%20Darkness), who open a seven-night arena run at [Glasgow's OVO Hydro on December 8](/en/concert/en-the-darkness-glasgow-20261208) and finish at [The O2 in London on December 16](/en/concert/en-the-darkness-london-20261216) — their first headline show at that venue, and their largest run in twenty years. Two weeks earlier, on November 27, they release [Crock Of Hits](/en/concert/en-the-darkness-crock-of-hits-20261127), the first greatest hits set they have curated themselves, drawing on eight studio albums since 2003 and adding a re-recorded *Masters Of The Universe* with Brian May.

The sequencing matters. The compilation is not a souvenir sold at the merch table after the fact; it is the thing that justifies booking rooms this size. A band whose most recent album, 2025's *Dreams On Toast*, reached number two in the UK is still choosing to frame December around the whole catalog rather than the new record. Brothers Osborne and A support all seven nights, which are spread across Glasgow, Leeds, Manchester, Birmingham, Cardiff, Brighton and London.

## Re-staging a specific past

The [Jonas Brothers](/en/concert/en-jonas-brothers-prudential-center-20261221) are doing something narrower and stranger. The Burning Up Tour All Over Again is not a hits tour; it is a named reconstruction of one particular 2008 tour, rebuilt for arenas eighteen years later. Rather than presenting the catalog as a menu, it presents one slice of it as an event. The run grew out of sold-out Madison Square Garden nights in August 2026 and expanded to 45 North American dates, closing at the Prudential Center in Newark on December 21 after stops including [the Hollywood Bowl on November 18](/en/concert/en-jonas-brothers-hollywood-bowl-20261118) and [Ball Arena in Denver on December 9](/en/concert/en-jonas-brothers-burning-up-all-over-again-denver-20261209).

[Backstreet Boys](/en/artist/Backstreet%20Boys) apply the same logic to a single album. Their Into The Millennium residency, built around 1999's *Millennium*, has run since July 2025 and passed 750,000 attendees across 47 sold-out nights; it extends into [a New Year's Eve show at Sphere in Las Vegas](/en/concert/en-backstreet-boys-into-the-millennium-nye-las-vegas-20261231) on December 31, with further dates on January 1 and 2. One album, one room, two and a half years.

## The anniversary version

At smaller scale, the anniversary supplies the occasion. [PENTAGON](/en/artist/PENTAGON) played [two Seoul nights on October 9 and 10](/en/concert/en-pentagon-101010-seoul-20261009), the second falling on the exact tenth anniversary of their 2016 debut, alongside their first release together in three years. Nice as Fuck — Jenny Lewis, Erika Spring and Tennessee Thomas — reunited for [eight dates opening in Pasadena on October 16](/en/concert/en-nice-as-fuck-10th-anniversary-pasadena-20261016), reissuing their 2016 debut to mark it — a catalog of exactly one record, which turns out to be enough. And [Iron Maiden](/en/artist/Iron%20Maiden) mark fifty years on December 11 with no tour at all: a [coloured-vinyl box set](/en/concert/en-iron-maiden-50th-anniversary-box-set-20261211) collecting their first nine albums through 1992's *Fear Of The Dark*, the catalog as object rather than performance.

## And the act moving the other way

[Supergrass](/en/artist/Supergrass) are the useful counterexample. Having spent the previous year touring the thirtieth anniversary of *I Should Coco*, Gaz Coombes has said the band will deliberately leave that format behind for their [thirteen-date UK run in spring 2027](/en/concert/en-supergrass-glasgow-20270427), pulling from all six albums instead. Demand suggests the wider catalog is the stronger draw: three extra nights were added after the originals sold, including [a third London show at the O2 Forum Kentish Town on May 9](/en/concert/en-supergrass-london-20270509).

That is the tension worth watching. A single-album show is finite by design — it can only be played until the anniversary passes. A catalog show has no expiry date, which is exactly why so many acts are building the back half of this year around one.
