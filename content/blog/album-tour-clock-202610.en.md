There is a tidy version of how this is supposed to work: an album comes out, the artist goes on the road behind it, the two things move together. Almost nothing in the next three weeks does that. Between September 22 and October 10, 2026, five new albums arrive and roughly a dozen tours open, and the distance between any given release and the shows attached to it ranges from six days to three months to never. Read as a group, that spread says a lot about what a tour is actually for now.

## The one that plays it straight

[Wynonna Judd releases The Hard Truth](/en/concert/en-wynonna-judd-the-hard-truth-20261002) on October 2 through ANTI- Records — her first solo studio album since 2016's *Wynonna & the Big Noise*, and the first she has co-written all the way through, with contributions from Patterson Hood of Drive-By Truckers, The War and Treaty and Iron & Wine. Six days later she [opens The Hard Truth Tour at New York's Bowery Ballroom](/en/concert/en-wynonna-judd-hard-truth-new-york-20261008), the first of fifteen theatre-scale dates that close November 21 in Gary, Indiana, with Nashville duo Troubadour Blue opening every announced night. A 575-capacity club in New York for an artist of that standing is not an accident of scheduling; it is the whole pitch. New songs, small rooms, no nostalgia package.

## The albums arriving alone

Four albums land with no tour attached in this window at all. [Brandi Carlile's *Returning To Myself (Sad Gay Version)*](/en/concert/en-brandi-carlile-returning-to-myself-sad-gay-version-20260925), out September 25 on Interscope and Lost Highway, is a 21-track expansion that reworks all ten songs from the original in a quieter register, four of them cut at Long Pond Studio in upstate New York with Aaron Dessner of The National. [Victoria Monét's *Frequency of Love*](/en/concert/en-victoria-monet-frequency-of-love-20261002) follows on October 2 — 22 tracks, her first full-length since the 2024 Best New Artist Grammy, led by the KAYTRANADA- and D'Mile-produced "Reach Out." October 9 brings two more: [Greta Van Fleet's fourth album *Palace For The People*](/en/concert/en-greta-van-fleet-palace-for-the-people-20261009), recorded in Tennessee with Mike Elizondo co-producing, and [Remi Wolf's *Mud*](/en/concert/en-remi-wolf-mud-20261009), eleven tracks assembled across Nashville, Big Bear, New York and Joshua Tree. Each is an album doing its own work first, with the touring conversation pushed to a later quarter.

## The tours catching up to something already out

The larger group this month is the reverse: shows arriving well after the release they support. [Steve Lacy plays Radio City Music Hall on October 10](/en/concert/en-steve-lacy-radio-city-20261010), his debut in the room, on a 27-city run that is his first headline tour since 2022 — behind *Oh yeah?*, which came out July 17. [Icona Pop open a short club run at Baby's All Right in Brooklyn](/en/concert/en-icona-pop-ritual-tour-brooklyn-20260925) on September 25 for *Ritual*, released August 14. And [Erykah Badu and The Alchemist close LIVE: A September Tour at the Hollywood Bowl](/en/concert/en-erykah-badu-alchemist-hollywood-bowl-20260929) on September 29, with Smino supporting in place of De La Soul — the first extended live outing behind an album that only arrived August 28. A two-month lag used to look like a missed window. Now it looks like the album buying itself time to find an audience before anyone is asked to pay for parking.

## The tour that gets there first

[Michelle Branch's Everywhere and Back Again Tour opens September 27 at The Showbox in Seattle](/en/concert/en-michelle-branch-everywhere-back-again-seattle-20260927), eighteen dates that run through mid-November — and the EP it supports, newly recorded versions of career-spanning songs, does not arrive until November 6 on BMG. The tour is the release campaign. [Audrey Hobert takes a similar shape from the other direction](/en/concert/en-audrey-hobert-staircase-to-stardom-brooklyn-20260929), opening the final leg of The Staircase To Stardom Tour at Brooklyn Paramount on September 29 in the biggest headline rooms of her career, a leg announced in late July and built around her debut album *Who's The Clown?*, with both weekends of Austin City Limits stacked on top.

## And the nights that need no record at all

Then there is everything selling nothing but the show. [Rush added Houston's Toyota Center on October 1](/en/concert/en-rush-fifty-something-houston-20261001) as one of four new single-night stops on the Fifty Something reunion run, with Anika Nilles on drums. [Neil Young and Willie Nelson share a bill in Bridgeport on September 22](/en/concert/en-neil-young-willie-nelson-bridgeport-20260922), the first of two co-headline dates announced in August. [BEAT — Adrian Belew, Tony Levin, Steve Vai and Terry Bozzio — launch a 40-date run in Santa Cruz](/en/concert/en-beat-fall-tour-santa-cruz-20261009) on October 9. [Monsta X bring THE X : NEXUS to the Infosys Theater at Madison Square Garden](/en/concert/en-monsta-x-nexus-newyork-20261006) on October 6. None of these are pegged to a release date, and none of them need to be.

The useful takeaway for anyone planning a fall calendar: an album release date is no longer a reliable predictor of when you can see the songs played. Check the tour separately. You can browse the full run of dates on the [calendar](/en) or by [artist](/en/artist).
