If you follow Korean music from outside Korea, most of what reaches you is idol touring: world tours, encores, fan meetings, the machinery of K-pop. But there is a second Korean concert calendar that rarely gets translated, and it peaks right now. Between late November and late January, Korea's homegrown singers, bands and rappers — artists who mostly don't tour abroad and don't need to — take over the country's arenas and halls for what Koreans simply call the year-end concert.

The year-end show is a ritual more than a tour. Audiences return to the same artist every winter, the runs are usually two or three nights in one room, and the tickets disappear months in advance. Here is how this year's season lines up, from a rock legend who started in 1968 to an indie songwriter playing her first arena.

## The Legends Take KSPO Dome

The biggest name of the season is also the oldest. [Cho Yong-pil](/en/artist/Cho%20Yong-pil), on stage since 1968, plays [three nights at KSPO Dome](/en/concert/en-cho-yong-pil-great-birth-seoul-20261127) with his longtime band The Great Birth on November 27, 28 and 29, each show running about 130 minutes. Tickets went on sale exclusively through YES24 Ticket in September, priced from 110,000 won to 176,000 won for VIP. For international readers, the closest comparison is a hometown arena residency by a songwriter whose catalog spans six decades, and a run like this one is the centerpiece of the Korean winter.

The same arena hosts a very different milestone one week earlier. Ballad singer [Roy Kim](/en/artist/Roy%20Kim) headlines KSPO Dome for the first time with his 2026-27 LIVE TOUR [R:O:Y]. The two original nights, [November 21](/en/concert/en-roy-kim-roy-live-tour-seoul-20261121) and [November 22](/en/concert/en-roy-kim-roy-live-tour-seoul-20261122), sold out after general sale, so a third show was added on [November 20](/en/concert/en-roy-kim-roy-live-tour-seoul-20261120). It follows four straight years of sold-out year-end concerts — exactly how the tradition works. An artist builds a loyal winter audience in smaller rooms, and eventually that audience outgrows them.

## Hip-Hop Has Its Own Rituals

Rap in Korea has a year-end tradition too. [Dynamic Duo](/en/artist/Dynamic%20Duo) hold their annual concert, titled Let's See Each Other Once in a While after a track from their seventh studio album, for [three nights at Olympic Hall](/en/concert/en-dynamic-duo-once-in-a-while-seoul-20261127) from November 27 to 29. The series has sold out every Seoul date for three years running since 2023, part of the proceeds goes to the welfare organization Love's Snail, and Busan and Daejeon dates follow in December.

At the other end of a long career, [Simon Dominic](/en/artist/Simon%20Dominic) — Ssamdi to many fans — finally headlines his first solo concert, [SIMON SAYS](/en/concert/en-simon-dominic-simon-says-seoul-20270109), at [YES24 Live Hall](/en/venue/YES24%20Live%20Hall%2C%20Seoul) on January 9 and 10. Standing tickets on the ground floor cost 121,000 won. For a rapper who has been part of the scene this long, a first solo headline show is overdue, and it makes this one of the more meaningful small-room bookings of the winter.

## Reunions and Returning Names

Some of the season's year-end bookings are about coming back. [Panic](/en/artist/Panic), the 1990s duo of Lee Juck and Kim Jin-pyo, return for a [three-night run at Kyung Hee University's Grand Peace Hall](/en/concert/en-panic-is-coming-again-seoul-20261204) from December 4 to 6, titled PANIC IS COMING, AGAIN. It follows their sold-out April reunion concerts, the pair's first in twenty years; tickets were capped at two per account across all three nights.

The same weekend, [Rain](/en/artist/Rain) closes his year with two shows called [THE SMOKE](/en/concert/en-rain-the-smoke-seoul-20261205) at Olympic Hall on December 5 and 6, with seats running from 110,000 won to 198,000 won for VVIP. Two weeks later, Super Junior's [KYUHYUN](/en/artist/KYUHYUN) launches his CONTINUE Asia tour at [Jangchung Arena](/en/concert/en-kyuhyun-continue-asia-tour-seoul-20261218) from December 18 to 20, named after the EP he releases on November 11. Later cities have not been announced, which makes the Seoul run the only confirmed stop for now.

## Trot and the Road Tour

Not every year-end show stays in Seoul. Trot star [Young Tak](/en/artist/Young%20Tak) runs the fifth season of his annual TAK SHOW across the country — Seoul, Jeonju and Daegu in late November, Incheon in December — before closing at [BEXCO in Busan](/en/concert/en-young-tak-tak-show5-busan-20270116) on January 16 and 17. Trot, a genre far older than K-pop, is one of the clearest examples of a domestic music world that stays almost invisible in English coverage while filling exhibition halls at home.

## The New Guard Moves Up

The most interesting stories of the season are the artists moving into bigger rooms. Singer-songwriter [HANRORO](/en/artist/HANRORO), known for guitar-driven songs about youthful restlessness since her 2022 debut single, plays her [first arena shows at Inspire Arena](/en/concert/en-hanroro-question-mark-incheon-20270102) near Incheon Airport on January 2 and 3 with her fifth solo concert, Question Mark. Tickets go on sale November 13 at 6 p.m. KST on NOL Ticket and Melon Ticket.

[HYUKOH](/en/artist/HYUKOH) open their [first solo world tour in about five years](/en/concert/en-hyukoh-33-tour-seoul-20270116) at Olympic Hall on January 16 and 17, ahead of their new album 33. This is the one homegrown booking of the winter with a clear international tail: after Seoul the band heads to Taipei, Tokyo, seven North American cities, London and Big Ears Festival. Seoul tickets open on Melon Ticket on November 12 at 8 p.m. KST.

And rock band [TOUCHED](/en/artist/TOUCHED) bring the fifth edition of their winter show HIGHLIGHT to [Korea University's Hwajung Gymnasium](/en/concert/en-touched-highlight-v-seoul-20270123) on January 23 and 24 — a date the band revealed at the end of their Grand Mint Festival 2026 set. Every attendee gets a light-up cheering bracelet, a small detail that shows how much the band-scene year-end show has borrowed from idol concert culture.

## How to Read the Season

Put together, Korea's year-end calendar runs on repetition rather than novelty. Dynamic Duo, Young Tak and TOUCHED all present numbered or annual editions; Roy Kim's arena debut grew out of four previous winters; Panic's December run exists because April sold out. For a visitor in Seoul this winter, that is the practical takeaway: these shows sell on loyalty, so the tickets still worth chasing are the ones opening this week — HYUKOH on November 12 and HANRORO on November 13.

For the full picture, including the idol tours and international visitors that share these same rooms, browse the [calendar](/en) or the [artist list](/en/artist). The year-end season is the best time of year to see what Korean music sounds like when it is playing mainly for itself.
