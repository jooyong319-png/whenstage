Fall gets discussed as a touring season. Routing, on-sales, which stadium sold out in what number of minutes. But the release calendar underneath it is doing something more interesting this year: between August 21 and October 30, eighteen albums arrive, and an unusual share of them are the first thing a given artist has put out in five, six, eleven, twenty-one years. It is a season built out of ended silences.

Here is how the ten Fridays break down, and what is worth clearing space for.

## Late August: Three Albums on One Friday, Then Two More

August 21 is the crowded one. [Brandon Flowers](/en/artist/Brandon%20Flowers) releases **Thrasher**, his third solo album and his first in over a decade — it follows 2010's *Flamingo* and 2015's *The Desired Effect*. The Killers frontman cut it in Nashville at Historic RCA Studio A with producers Shawn Everett and Jonathan Rado, and the personnel list tells you where it is headed: guitarist David Rawlings, pedal-steel player Bruce Bouton, and 85-year-old harmonica veteran Charlie McCoy. A country record, in other words, made with people who have been making country records longer than Flowers has been alive.

The same day, [Weezer](/en/artist/Weezer) put out their twentieth studio album — self-titled, universally called **The Gold Album**, their first full-length since *Van Weezer* in 2021. It is the first of the colour-coded self-titled records to skip a band photo on the sleeve, which after twenty albums counts as a statement. Kenneth Blume and Klas Ahlund produced; *Shine Again*, *We Might as Well Be Strangers* and *C.E.O.* arrived ahead of it.

Tucked into the same Friday at a very different scale: Brooklyn post-punk duo Um, Jennifer? release the EP **Dirty Twice** on Final Girl. Fig and Eli wrote and recorded all of it themselves, splitting vocals, guitar, bass and drums between them with Teddy O'Mara producing. They spend the autumn on the road supporting Slothrust.

A week later, on [August 28](/en/concert/en-jim-james-wowed-out-20260828), [Jim James](/en/artist/Jim%20James) releases **Wowed Out**. The My Morning Jacket frontman has described it as drawing on Alice Coltrane's spiritual recordings — he wanted the songs to feel like a journey that takes the listener somewhere rewarding. A solo acoustic tour follows from November 28, 2026 into April 2027. Sharing the date is the [long-delayed Erykah Badu and The Alchemist joint album](/en/concert/en-erykah-badu-alchemist-album-20260828), recorded over roughly eighteen months across Dallas and Los Angeles, announced with the single *Witch Doctor* and, at announcement, still without a title or cover art — the Bandcamp listing was left deliberately untitled.

## Early September: A Debut and a Ninth Album That Refused to Ship

September 4 pairs a first record with a very late one. [ADÉLA](/en/artist/AD%C3%89LA), the Bratislava-born pop provocateur, releases her debut **PRIMA** on Capitol, executive-produced by Dylan Brady and Blake Slatkin, trailed by *Ain't In LA* — a single and video filmed in her hometown. [Kasabian](/en/artist/Kasabian) release **ACT III**, their ninth, pushed back from an original July 17 date because the band decided they could not send it out "without getting everything just right." Serge Pizzorno again handles lead vocals, as he has since Tom Meighan's 2020 departure.

Then [Chrome Sparks](/en/artist/Chrome%20Sparks) on September 11 with **Ball Of Light**, producer Jeremy Malvin's first full-length under the name since his 2018 self-titled debut. Twelve tracks, led by *Sleeper*, which somehow puts Khalid's vocal and Jónsi of Sigur Rós over the same cinematic production. Malvin has called the album the sound of finding himself again after years away.

## Mid-September: Two Kinds of Beginning

September 18 sets a debut against a fourth album, and both are about turning something difficult into a record. [GAYLE](/en/artist/GAYLE) — the *abcdefu* breakout, five years on from the viral moment — finally delivers a proper debut, the thirteen-track **Observing Chaos** on Atlantic, led by *Loving You Is* and the summery *junebug!*. [Orville Peck](/en/concert/en-orville-peck-mule-20260918) releases **Mule**, thirteen tracks tracing the year that reshaped his life: addiction, the pursuit of sobriety, the slow work of rebuilding. Lead single *Too Little, Too Late* was written with Dan Wilson and Steve Rusch and produced by Jay Joyce, and the album includes *Prologue (Eulogy Of A Man Turned Mule)*, a collaboration with Emmylou Harris, Noah Cyrus and [Allison Russell](/en/artist/Allison%20Russell).

On September 25, [Brandi Carlile](/en/artist/Brandi%20Carlile) does something rarer: she reissues her own album against itself. **Returning To Myself (Sad Gay Version)** is a 21-track expanded edition that reworks all ten original songs in a quieter register, four of them co-produced at Long Pond Studio in upstate New York with Aaron Dessner of The National. A new reading of *Human* featuring Jon Hopkins arrived as the preview.

## October: The Long Gaps

October is where the season's real theme surfaces. [Victoria Monét](/en/artist/Victoria%20Mon%C3%A9t) opens the month on October 2 with **Frequency of Love**, a 22-track sophomore album and her first full-length since the 2024 Best New Artist Grammy — led by the KAYTRANADA- and D'Mile-produced *Reach Out*, with Camper executive producing. The same day, [Wynonna Judd](/en/artist/Wynonna%20Judd) releases **The Hard Truth** on ANTI-, her first solo studio album since 2016 and the first she has co-written throughout, with Patterson Hood of Drive-By Truckers, The War and Treaty and Iron & Wine contributing. A fifteen-date headlining run follows in October and November.

[Greta Van Fleet](/en/artist/Greta%20Van%20Fleet) return October 9 with **Palace For The People**, recorded in Tennessee in spring 2026 and co-produced with Mike Elizondo, whose credits run from Turnstile to Fiona Apple — an unexpected pairing, and the band's first album after an extended break from the road.

October 16 is the best Friday of the month. [Rachel Chinouriri](/en/concert/en-rachel-chinouriri-i-think-i-spoke-too-soon-20261016) releases her second album, **I think I spoke too soon**, written and recorded across London, Hereford and Los Angeles with returning collaborators alongside newcomers Lucy Dacus and Eli Teplin. [The Avalanches](/en/artist/The%20Avalanches) release **No Bad Memories**, their fourth and first since 2020's *We Will Always Love You*, with contributions from Karen O, Jamie xx, Nikki Nair, Jessy Lanza and Prentiss scattered across it.

The most improbable release of the season lands October 23: [Tony Iommi](/en/artist/Tony%20Iommi)'s **From the Dark**, his first solo album in 21 years and his first full-length of any kind since Black Sabbath's *13* in 2013. Eight tracks, produced with longtime collaborator Mike Exeter, with Jorn Lande on vocals. Alongside it, [Claud](/en/artist/Claud) releases **Me and the Music** on Dead Oceans, their third and first since 2023's *Supermodels*.

The month closes October 30 with [THAO](/en/artist/THAO)'s **Show Me**, a return to Kill Rock Stars and her first album in six years, co-produced with Merrill Garbus and Nate Brenner of Tune-Yards and featuring Neko Case, The Linda Lindas and Black Pumas guitarist Adrian Quesada. A short West Coast and East Coast run follows in November.

## What the Calendar Is Actually Telling You

Two things, if you line all eighteen up.

The first is the gaps. Twenty-one years for Iommi. Eleven for Flowers. Ten for Wynonna Judd. Eight for Chrome Sparks. Six for THAO and for The Avalanches. These are not artists who lost momentum and are chasing it; they are mostly people who stopped on purpose and are coming back on their own schedule, to labels — ANTI-, Kill Rock Stars, Dead Oceans — that are built to absorb that timeline.

The second is who else is in the room. Almost none of these records were made alone. Emmylou Harris and Noah Cyrus on an Orville Peck track. Aaron Dessner producing Brandi Carlile. Lucy Dacus on Rachel Chinouriri. Tune-Yards, Neko Case and The Linda Lindas on a THAO album. Khalid and Jónsi on the same Chrome Sparks single. Charlie McCoy, at 85, playing harmonica on a Killers frontman's country record. The exception proves it — Um, Jennifer? made theirs as a two-person operation, and that is the one that reads as unusual.

If you want to track the on-sales that follow these records rather than the records themselves, most of the artists above have fall dates on the [concert calendar](/en), and the [full artist index](/en/artist) will show you where each one is playing.
