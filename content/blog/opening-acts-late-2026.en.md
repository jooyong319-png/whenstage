Almost everyone books a tour by the headliner. You see a name you like, you check whether it is coming to your city, you buy. The second line of the poster — the support act, the special guest, the band playing at 7:15 to a half-full room — barely registers.

It should. Across the shows landing between October 2026 and early 2027, the support slot is doing more explaining than the headline. It tells you whether a tour is a nostalgia package or a genuine risk, whether the headliner is building a scene or renting one, and often whether the show is two hours long or three. Here is what the undercard looks like this season.

## When there is no support act, because there are two headliners

The most striking shows of the next three months have abandoned the hierarchy entirely.

[Usher and Chris Brown bring The R&B Tour to MetLife Stadium on October 15](/en/concert/en-usher-chris-brown-rb-tour-east-rutherford-20261015), one of eight stadium dates added in August after the co-headline run sold out its opening stretch. What makes it unusual is the staging: instead of separate headline sets, the two singers alternate across nine cinematic acts in a nearly three-hour production, with Mario, Eric Bellinger and Tank joining them onstage. Nobody is the support act. The 59-date Live Nation trek is built as one show, not two stacked on top of each other.

[ZZ Top and Cheap Trick share several North American dates on The Big One! Tour](/en/concert/en-zz-top-the-big-one-nashville-20261008), including Nashville's Ascend Amphitheater on October 8 — two Rock and Roll Hall of Fame inductees splitting a bill rather than one carrying the other. [Jimmy Eat World and The Format co-headline the Hollywood Bowl on November 7](/en/concert/en-jimmy-eat-world-the-format-hollywood-bowl-20261107), the first time the two Arizona bands have shared a stage, each playing an anniversary album front to back: *Bleed American* at twenty-five, *Dog Problems* at twenty. [Amorphis and Insomnium open a 23-date North American run in Boston on November 17](/en/concert/en-amorphis-insomnium-boston-20261117) and swap headline duties across the whole tour. And in the new year, [System of a Down and Faith No More play four stadiums across Australia and New Zealand](/en/concert/en-system-of-a-down-faith-no-more-sydney-20270122), a bill that only exists because both bands needed the other to justify a stadium.

## When the support act is the scene

Metal keeps the clearest version of this. [Amon Amarth launch The Allfather Awakens tour at Manchester's O2 Apollo on October 9](/en/concert/en-amon-amarth-o2-apollo-manchester-20261009) with Orbit Culture and Soilwork below them — an entirely Swedish bill, playing England. That is not a booking convenience; it is a statement about where the music comes from. The Amorphis and Insomnium run does something adjacent by carrying Texas melodic death metal band Hinayana on every one of its 23 dates rather than rotating regional support, which turns a co-headline tour into a travelling package with a third act who gets to build an audience across a continent.

[Nia Archives plays Alexandra Palace on November 26](/en/concert/en-nia-archives-emotional-world-tour-london-20261126) with three names under hers — Cannelle, NEW YORK and Trickpony — on a tour that opened in Japan before crossing North America and Europe. A jungle producer filling the undercard with three acts instead of one is doing the same thing a festival stage does, inside a single ticket.

## When the support act is family, or the hometown

[The Jonas Brothers reach the Hollywood Bowl on November 18](/en/concert/en-jonas-brothers-hollywood-bowl-20261118) with All Time Low added to a bill that otherwise travels with Magnus Ferrell and Deleasa on all 45 North American arena stops. The tour revisits the 2008 run that broke the band nationally, and the support reflects that: mostly people already in the family orbit, with one pop-punk name dropped in for the biggest room of the leg.

Scale down and the logic holds. [Wynonna Judd opens The Hard Truth Tour at New York's Bowery Ballroom on October 8](/en/concert/en-wynonna-judd-hard-truth-new-york-20261008), a 575-capacity club, with Nashville duo Troubadour Blue opening every announced date of the fifteen-show run. [Quadeca's 23-date Life 1 Tour](/en/concert/en-quadeca-life-1-tour-seattle-20261106), which stops at Seattle's Showbox on November 6, keeps Derby and Oleka on the whole thing. Consistency is the point — a small headliner who changes openers every night is a headliner nobody is building anything with.

## When the support act changes, and why

[Oso Oso opens a 32-date North American run in Allston on October 7](/en/concert/en-oso-oso-exchange-of-information-allston-20261007) with Leisure Hour and Bad Luck on the eastern leg, then swaps to Liquid Mike and Bugsy for the second leg starting in Columbus. Two legs, two entirely different rooms' worth of bands, one headliner.

Crossing a border changes it too. [The Weeknd's two Goyang Stadium nights on October 7 and 8](/en/concert/en-the-weeknd-after-hours-til-dawn-seoul-20261007) are opened by Japanese duo Creepy Nuts — a regional pairing that would never appear on the North American leg. [Tame Impala's Deadbeat Tour closes at home in Perth on October 24](/en/concert/en-tame-impala-deadbeat-perth-20261024) with Australian producer Ninajirachi supporting every date. [The Pussycat Dolls close their European leg at London's O2 on October 13](/en/concert/en-pussycat-dolls-pcd-forever-london-20261013) with Lil' Kim and Deja Vu billed as special guests rather than support, which is a different promise: shorter sets, probably a shared stage moment.

## The practical version

If you are deciding between two shows, the undercard is the cheapest signal available. A co-headline bill usually means a longer night and two full productions. A single support act carried across an entire tour usually means the headliner has something invested in them, and the set will be worth arriving early for. A rotating local opener means the doors time is negotiable.

Most of the shows above are theatre or arena scale, and the names below the headline change by market. Check the bill for your date specifically on the [calendar](/en) or by [artist](/en/artist) before you decide what time to show up — and if you are new to how on-sales and presales work in these markets, the [guide](/en/guide) covers the mechanics.
